A job is one piece of work for one client, like building 5,000 holiday gift kits. One job has one kind of unit and one price. If you build three kit versions at three prices, make three jobs. The new job page takes you through three steps and shows the margin the job should earn before you save it.

For the fastest way in, use **Quick add a job** at the top of the Jobs list. See [Use the Jobs list](/docs/pulse/jobs-list).

## Before you start

- Adding a job needs a manager, admin or owner. Other roles see **Your role cannot add jobs**.
- A labor rate makes the margin preview right. See [Set labor classes and rates](/docs/pulse/labor-rates).

## Start the job

1. Go to **Jobs** and select **New job**, or press `n`.
2. If Pulse has something to start from, the **Start faster** panel offers it. See the table below.
3. Pick one, or skip the panel and type the job in. A filled form says **Filled in from**, with **Start over** to clear it.

| Start from | What it brings |
| --- | --- |
| **A won deal** | From your CRM. Brings the name, the client and the deal value. |
| **A warehouse work order** | From your WMS. Brings the kit, the client and the units to build. |
| **A past job** | Copies the client, the price and the plan. No entries come along. |

## Step 1: Basics

| Field | What to type |
| --- | --- |
| **Client** | Who the work is for. Type a new name and Pulse adds the client when you save. |
| **Job name** | What you would call it on the floor. |
| **Kind of work** | The job type. It sets the unit word, the usual pace and the target margin. |
| **How long does it run?** | **One time, with an end** or **Ongoing, month after month**. |
| **Units to make** | The whole job, start to finish. For an ongoing job this reads **Units in a usual month**. |
| **What do you call one?** | Kits, boxes, pallets, cases. Shows next to every count. |
| **Start date** and **Due date** | Pulse warns you when the pace will miss the due date. |
| **Site** | The building the work runs in. |
| **Owner** | Who gets the alerts for this job. |

Select **Next: price and plan**.

## Step 2: Price and plan

| Field | What to type |
| --- | --- |
| **How do you charge?** | **Price per unit**, **One fixed price** or **Price per labor hour**. Then the price, up to 4 decimal places. |
| **Target margin** | The margin you want after all costs. |
| **What do you know about the labor?** | **The hours it will take** (labor hours for the whole job, people times hours) or **How fast one person works** (units one person finishes in an hour). A pace you type becomes the target pace for the job. Pulse shows how past jobs of this kind ran when it can. |
| **Setup hours** | Time that makes no units. |
| **Who does the work?** | A labor class, which sets the real cost of an hour. You can type a **Real cost of an hour** for this plan only. |
| **Who buys the parts?** | **We do** or **The client does**. When you buy them, type the **Material cost for one** unit. |
| **Other costs** | Freight in and the like, one row each. |
| **Extra services** | Services sold on top, each with how many, the price each and your cost each. |

The **Margin preview** updates as you type: revenue, labor, material, other costs, time not on a job, total cost, profit, **Job margin**, **Margin before time not on a job** and **Breakeven**. It says whether the margin is at or above your target, and the price that would reach it.

Select **Next: review**, or **Skip the plan for now**.

## Step 3: Review

1. Check the summary under **Check it over**.
2. Under **Last details**, answer **Start it now?**. Active jobs show on the Log work page today. Planned jobs wait until you start them.
3. Optionally add a **Client PO number** and **Notes for the team**.
4. Select **Create the job**.

The numbers you typed become the job's quote, so plan vs actual starts from day one. See [Add a quote or a budget](/docs/pulse/quotes-and-budgets).

## Worked example (made-up figures)

You quote 5,000 kits at $2.75 each, plan 125 labor hours at a real cost of $21.76 an hour, and buy $0.45 of parts per kit.

- Revenue: 5,000 x $2.75 = $13,750.00
- Labor: 125 x $21.76 = $2,720.00
- Material: 5,000 x $0.45 = $2,250.00
- Before time not on a job: $13,750.00 less $4,970.00 = $8,780.00

The preview then takes away your usual share of time not on a job to show the job margin.
