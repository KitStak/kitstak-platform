One set of password rules applies everywhere you set a password: sign up, an invite, a reset, a forced change and **My account**. The rules are on screen before you type, and each one turns green as you meet it. The server checks the same rules again, so the screen never says yes to a password the server refuses.

## The four rules

A password needs all four:

| Rule on screen | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **10 or more characters** | At least 10 characters long. |
| **A capital and a small letter** | At least one A to Z and one a to z. |
| **A number** | At least one digit, 0 to 9. |
| **A symbol, like ! or #** | At least one character that is not a letter or a number. A space counts. |

> **Tip:** 15 or more characters is enough by itself. A few words work well, like `kettle orbit maple seven`. A password that long needs no capital, number or symbol.

The longest password KitStak takes is 72 characters. Some letters and symbols take more room than one character, so a very long password with them can be refused with **Use 72 characters or fewer.**

## Passwords that are always refused

At any length, KitStak refuses a password that a guessing program tries first:

- Almost one character, like `aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa`.
- One short piece said again and again, like `passwordpassword`.
- Straight runs on the keyboard or in the alphabet, like `1234`, `qwerty` or `abcdef`.
- A common password, even dressed up with digits, symbols, capitals or look alike characters, like `P@ssw0rd2024`.
- A password built around the word KitStak or your own company name.
- A password that contains the part of your email before the @, when that part is 3 or more characters.

## What the messages mean

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Your password still needs a number and a symbol, like ! or #. | Add what is named. Every missing rule is named at once. |
| Or make it 15 characters or longer. | Shown once you have 10 characters: a longer password skips the other rules. |
| That one is too easy to guess. Try a few words that do not go together. | Pick words that do not belong together. |
| Do not use your company name in your password. | Take the company name out. |
| Do not use the word KitStak in your password. | Take the word KitStak out. |
| Do not use your email name in your password. | Take out the part of your email before the @. |
| Choose a password that is different from the one you were given. | On a forced change, the new password cannot be the temporary one. |

The warning under the field follows what you type, so it is never about an older try. If you change the email or company after the password, the warning checks again.

## The strength meter

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Too weak** | Rules are missing, or it is a password a guessing program tries first. |
| **Weak** or **Fair** | Some rules are met. |
| **Good** | All four rules are met, or the password is 15 or more characters. |
| **Strong** | Good, and 20 or more characters, or 14 or more with a capital, a number and a symbol. |

The meter is a guide. The four rules decide.
